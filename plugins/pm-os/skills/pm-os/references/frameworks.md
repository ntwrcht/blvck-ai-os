# Frameworks

Operational definitions the pm-os workflows apply. These are the PM's declared frameworks — don't substitute others without being asked.

## RICE (prioritization)
Score = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort.
- Reach: users or events per period, stated unit, from data where possible
- Impact on a named metric: 3 massive / 2 high / 1 medium / 0.5 low / 0.25 minimal
- Confidence: 100% data / 80% strong signal / 50% informed guess; <50% means the item needs discovery, not a score
- Effort: person-months from engineering; never invent it
Failure modes to catch: reach counted twice via overlapping segments; impact scored on a metric nobody tracks; effort optimism.

## Jobs-to-be-Done (discovery)
Job statement: When [situation], [user] wants to [motivation], so they can [expected outcome].
Four forces on switching: push of the problem, pull of the new, anxiety of the new, habit of the present.
Interview discipline: past behavior only; timeline reconstruction (first thought → passive → active → decision → first use); quotes verbatim.

## NSM + L1/L2 metrics tree
NSM test: measures value delivered to users, correlates with revenue, the team can move it.
L1 = 3–5 direct inputs (typically activation, engagement, retention/expansion). L2 = operational drivers under each L1.
Every roadmap item names its L1/L2. Always define ≥1 counter-metric so the NSM can't be gamed.

## Pyramid principle (communication)
Conclusion first, then grouped rationale, then evidence. The ask appears in the first three lines of any document or update. If a reader stops after the first paragraph, they still know what you want.
